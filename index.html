📖 The Truth Behind Renting vs Buying a Home

The conventional wisdom that "renting is throwing money away" ignores the massive front-loaded unrecoverable costs of real estate ownership:

  • Unrecoverable Housing Costs: Neither renting nor buying is 100% equity building. Rent pays for shelter. Homeowners pay substantial "unrecoverable costs" including mortgage interest, property taxes (1-2%), insurance, maintenance (1% rule), and 6% seller closing commissions.
  • The 5% Rule of Real Estate: If your monthly rent is lower than 5% of the purchase price divided by 12, renting and investing the difference in index funds is often mathematically superior to buying.
  • Mobility and Geographic Arbitrage: Buying anchors you to a single physical labor market. Renters retain the flexibility to relocate freely for career promotions or salary increases without incurring 6% transaction fees and closing costs.

References: Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) Home Price Index; Case-Shiller Real Estate Index; Ben Felix 5% Housing Rule.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

When does buying a home become financially better than renting?

Buying typically breaks even against renting after 4 to 7 years. Over shorter horizons, upfront transaction costs and mortgage interest outweigh home equity accumulation.

What hidden costs of homeownership should I include?

Always factor in annual property taxes (1% to 2%), homeowner insurance, HOA fees, and recurring maintenance and repairs (budget 1% of home value annually).

How does the opportunity cost of down payment investing work?

Renters who invest their down payment and monthly savings in low-cost index funds compounding at 7% can build substantial liquid net worth that rivals real estate equity.
